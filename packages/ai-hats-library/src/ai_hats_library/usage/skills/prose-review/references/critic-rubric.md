# Editor's report — shape and criteria

The report is a proposal to the author. Every finding quotes the line it is
about, so the author can find it, judge it and accept or refuse it without
re-reading the whole text. Axes go in this order; the first three decide
whether the text works at all, the rest polish one that does.

The headings below are written in the report's language — the text's own. What
does not change with the language: the axes, their order, and the quoted line.

## Shape

```markdown
## Purpose

<one sentence: who reads this, what they leave with>

## Essence

- point surfaces at: > "…" (para N) — above it: keep / cut / move to top

## Repetition

- "…" ↔ > "…" — keep the first; the second adds <nothing / only X>

## Value per section

| Section | Reader gets | Verdict                      |
| ------- | ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| <h2>    | <one line>  | keep / merge into <h2> / cut |

## Style — <style-ru | style-en>

- "…" — <rule from the reference>

## Terms & voice

- reference: <name> | none — style left alone
- "…" — <term drifts from the glossary / voice flattened>

## Verdict

ship | one more round | rework the structure — <one line why>
```

## Criteria per axis

**Purpose.** One sentence, two halves: the reader, and what changes for them.
"Engineers who run agents in a monorepo leave knowing why worktrees, not
branches, keep agents from breaking each other's builds." A purpose that names
only a topic ("about worktrees") is not a purpose.

**Essence.** The purpose's substance should be the first thing the reader
meets — the first paragraph, or the first line under the first heading. Quote
where it actually appears. The material above that line is either setup the
reader needs to understand the point (keep) or a run-up (cut or move below).

**Repetition.** Two passages count as one idea in two dresses when a reader
who skipped the second would lose nothing. Quote both, keep the more concrete
one. Restating the introduction as a conclusion is the common case.

**Value per section.** For each heading, write what the reader gets in one
line, in the reader's terms ("how to pick the isolation mode"), not the
author's ("overview"). A section whose line is empty or repeats another
section's is merged or cut. A section whose line is fine but whose heading
says something else gets a heading finding.

**Style.** Findings come only from the language reference, quoted next to the
rule. Taste is not a finding; a rule the reference does not carry is not a
finding either — propose it for the reference instead.

**Terms & voice.** Terms: one name per thing, the glossary's name if there is
one. Voice: the author's sentence rhythm, person, register and signature moves
stay; a finding here names a place where the *draft itself* drifts from the
reference, never a place where the reference differs from the editor's taste.

**Verdict.** One of three, one line of reason. "Ship" means the first three
axes have no findings left. "One more round" means findings are local.
"Rework the structure" means the purpose sentence and the section table
disagree.
